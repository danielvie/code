# Goal

Reach shared understanding of the project's domain language, what the project is, what it must achieve, and why that goal is justified.

# Method

## 1. Inspect and frame

Read available project files before questioning the user. Determine what the project does, how it describes itself, whether an explicit goal exists, and what understanding is being sought.

Treat project evidence, user statements, and your interpretations as distinct. Surface contradictions between them. Begin with the largest unresolved issue affecting the project's purpose or goal.

## 2. Establish domain language

Shared understanding requires shared language. If the same words carry different meanings, questions and answers can appear aligned while describing different models.

Use `/domain-modeling` to read and maintain `CONTEXT.md`, sharpen vague or overloaded terms, resolve conflicting definitions, and record the canonical domain language. Return to the inquiry once the terms relevant to the current question are clear.

## 3. Ask

Ask one focused question at a time. Choose the type most likely to advance understanding:

- **Meaning:** What does an important term mean here?
- **Purpose:** What outcome is sought, and for whom?
- **Assumption:** What must be true for this claim to hold?
- **Evidence:** What supports or contradicts the claim?
- **Perspective:** How would another stakeholder describe the problem?
- **Alternative:** What other explanation or goal could fit the evidence?
- **Implication:** What follows if the claim is accepted?
- **Boundary:** What is explicitly outside the project or goal?
- **Criterion:** What observable result would demonstrate success?
- **Priority:** Which tradeoff matters when goals conflict?

Prefer short questions. Avoid compound, rhetorical, and leading questions.

Do not replace inquiry with recommendations. Offer a recommendation only when asked or after the relevant reasoning is understood.

## 4. Reflect

After each answer:

1. Restate the resulting understanding concisely.
2. Identify any assumption, ambiguity, or contradiction still present.
3. Ask the next highest-value question.

Ask for confirmation only when the restatement could materially change the meaning. Do not repeatedly reconfirm settled points.

## 5. Challenge

Test conclusions with counterexamples, edge cases, alternative interpretations, and consequences when they reveal whether the reasoning holds.

Challenge the reasoning, not the user. Do not lead toward a predetermined conclusion or manufacture objections after a conclusion is adequately supported.

## 6. Conclude

Shared understanding is reached when:

- the project's purpose and intended beneficiaries are understood
- important terms have clear meanings
- the goal is concise and coherent
- assumptions and supporting evidence are visible
- success criteria and boundaries are understood
- contradictions with the current project are resolved or explicit
- remaining uncertainty or disagreement is explicit
- no open question is likely to materially change the understanding

Stop asking questions and summarize the shared understanding. Do not force agreement where evidence or priorities do not resolve a difference.
